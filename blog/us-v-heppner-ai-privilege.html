核心摘要Executive Summary

2026年2月，纽约南区联邦法院Rakoff法官作出历史性裁定：被告Heppner使用公开AI工具Claude生成的31份法律策略文件，不受律师-客户特权（attorney-client privilege）和工作成果保护（work product doctrine）。这是美国联邦法院首次就AI与法律特权问题作出明确裁决，对每一个使用AI工具的律师和客户都敲响了警钟。

In February 2026, Judge Rakoff of the Southern District of New York made a landmark ruling: 31 legal strategy documents generated by defendant Heppner using the public AI tool Claude are not protected by attorney-client privilege or the work product doctrine. This is the first federal court ruling squarely addressing AI and legal privilege — a wake-up call for every lawyer and client using AI tools.

判决核心：用公开AI聊天等于向全世界公开你的秘密

Rakoff法官的逻辑很简单：你在公开AI平台上输入的任何内容，根据平台的隐私政策，可能被用于模型训练，可能被披露给第三方甚至政府机构。当"保密性"这个前提被摧毁，律师-客户特权和工作成果保护就失去了存在的基础。

一案件背景：一个CEO、一场欺诈、和31份AI文件

Bradley Heppner是GWG Holdings（一家纽约上市公司）的董事长。2025年11月，他因证券欺诈、电信欺诈、共谋、向审计师撒谎以及伪造记录等多项罪名被起诉。检察官指控他通过自己控制的壳公司Highland Consolidated从GWG Holdings中非法转移资金。

案件被分配给了联邦法官Jed S. Rakoff——纽约南区最资深、最以敢于做出开创性裁决著称的法官之一。

关键转折发生在联邦探员搜查Heppner的设备时：他们发现了31份使用Anthropic的Claude AI工具生成的文件。这些文件的内容令人震惊——Heppner在收到大陪审团传票并聘请律师后，自行使用Claude分析自己的案件、制定辩护策略、评估法律论点。

Heppner的辩护律师（来自顶级律所Quinn Emanuel）立即主张这31份文件受律师-客户特权和工作成果保护，要求排除在证据之外。检察官反对。由此引发了AI时代最重要的法律特权之争。

三Rakoff法官的三重否定：为什么AI文件全部出局

辩方提出了两条保护路径：律师-客户特权和工作成果保护。Rakoff法官对每一条都进行了严格的法律测试，然后逐一击破。

🔒

测试一：律师-客户特权 — 保密性要求

律师-客户特权的经典三要素：(1) 通讯仅在特权当事人之间进行；(2) 目的是获取法律意见；(3) 通讯保持保密。

Rakoff法官的致命一击：Heppner将信息输入Claude，就等于向第三方披露。Anthropic的隐私政策明确写道：用户输入可能被用于模型训练，且可能被披露给第三方甚至政府监管机构。

法官的比喻很直白：这就像在酒吧里跟朋友讨论自己的案件——你不能事后主张这些对话受律师-客户特权保护。

❌ 保密性被摧毁 → 特权不成立
👥

测试二：律师-客户特权 — 通讯对象要求

律师-客户特权只保护客户与律师之间的通讯。Rakoff法官指出："Claude不是律师"，不具备律师的信义义务和职业纪律约束，与用户之间不存在"基于信任的人际关系"。

更关键的是，Claude自己都声明："I'm not a lawyer and can't provide formal legal advice."——AI工具自己都不认为自己是律师。

辩方试图论证这些文件是为了"整合思路后与律师沟通"，但法官援引了经典原则：将非特权文件事后发送给律师，并不能使其变成特权文件。你不能通过"抄送律师"来给非特权文件镀金。

❌ 通讯对象不合格 → 特权不成立
📝

测试三：工作成果保护 — 律师指导要求

工作成果保护（work product doctrine）要求材料是"由律师或在律师指导下准备的"（prepared by or at the behest of counsel），且是为预期诉讼而准备。

Heppner的致命问题：这31份文件完全是他自己主动使用Claude生成的，没有任何证据表明他的律师指导他这么做。他只是一个客户，独立地使用了一个AI工具。

法官裁定：客户自行创建的文件，即使与诉讼相关，如果不是在律师指导下完成的，就不属于工作成果。AI工具更不能替代律师的"指导"角色。

⚠ 非律师指导 → 工作成果保护不适用

"向公开AI平台输入保密信息，就像在时代广场的大屏幕上展示你的辩护策略——你不能事后主张没人看到。"

根据Heppner案的裁判逻辑，以下流程图可以帮助律师和客户快速判断自己使用AI工具时的特权风险：

查看平台的隐私政策和服务条款

没有律师的书面指示。工作成果保护很可能不适用。

有律师的明确指示和记录。可能获得工作成果保护，但仍需确保平台保密性。

五消费级AI vs 企业级AI：一字之差，天壤之别

Heppner案的裁判逻辑高度依赖于Anthropic消费级产品的具体隐私政策。Rakoff法官也承认，不同的AI工具、不同的使用条款"可以被不同对待"。这意味着，使用工具的类型可能直接决定特权的生死。

🔴

消费级AI（高风险）

❌ 用户输入可能被用于模型训练
❌ 数据可能被共享给第三方
❌ 隐私政策允许向政府披露
❌ 无合同级别的保密义务
❌ Heppner案已明确裁定不受保护
🟢

企业级AI（更安全）

✅ 合同承诺不使用输入训练模型
✅ 数据隔离，不与第三方共享
✅ 企业级加密和访问控制
✅ 可签订保密协议（NDA/DPA）
⚠ 尚无判例明确确认受保护（但更有利）

还有第三种选择：本地部署/边缘计算的AI模型（如在自己的服务器上运行开源模型）。由于数据完全不离开内部网络，不存在"第三方披露"问题，最有可能维持特权保护。

六律师、客户和企业的应对清单

🚨

立即停止在公开AI平台输入案件保密信息

这是Heppner案最直接的教训。无论你是律师还是客户，在ChatGPT、Claude公开版、Gemini等消费级AI上输入任何与诉讼、调查相关的保密信息，特权保护几乎确定丧失。

📜

审查你使用的每一个AI工具的隐私政策

重点关注三个问题：输入数据是否被用于训练？是否可能被共享给第三方？是否可能被披露给政府机构？如果任何一个答案是"是"，就不要输入保密信息。

🏢

律所和企业法务：尽快采购企业级AI工具

企业版API（如Anthropic Enterprise、OpenAI Enterprise、Azure OpenAI）提供合同级别的保密承诺。虽然还没有判例明确确认企业级AI的特权保护，但它们在法律论证上远比消费级产品有利。

📝

建立"AI使用指令"文档记录机制

如果律师指导客户使用AI工具辅助准备材料，必须留下书面记录（邮件、备忘录）。这是维护工作成果保护的关键——Heppner案中，恰恰是因为缺乏律师指导的证据，工作成果保护才未能成立。

📖

更新特权审查日志（privilege log）

如果你在诉讼中使用了AI工具，特权日志应明确记录：使用了哪种AI工具、该工具的保密性承诺、使用是否在律师指导下进行、以及输入内容是否包含律师-客户通讯。

⚠

警告客户：不要把律师的建议输入公开AI

这是一个容易被忽视的风险：如果客户将律师给出的法律意见"转述"给公开AI寻求分析，可能导致原本受特权保护的律师-客户通讯因"自愿披露给第三方"而丧失特权。这不仅会暴露AI生成文件，还可能追溯性地摧毁原始律师通讯的特权。

七这个判决的深远影响

🔎

证据开示范围扩大

对方律师可能开始要求开示所有AI生成文件，作为发现程序的标准请求。

💻

法律科技行业重塑

企业级法律AI工具（如Harvey、CoCounsel）将获得巨大竞争优势——因为它们是"保密"的。

👥

Pro Se诉讼人面临更大风险

没有律师的个人更依赖公开AI，但他们的AI交互可能全部被要求作为证据披露。

🎒

法学院和律师继续教育必须更新

AI特权问题必须成为法律伦理课程的核心内容，律师需要理解技术才能保护客户。

八未解之谜：法律的灰色地带

Heppner案虽然具有里程碑意义，但它留下了几个重要的未决问题：

Kovel例外是否适用？在传统法律中，如果律师指示客户与第三方专家（如会计师、翻译）沟通以促进律师-客户通讯，这些与第三方的通讯也受特权保护（即Kovel例外）。如果律师明确指示客户使用AI工具翻译文件或分析财务数据，是否也能获得Kovel保护？法院没有给出答案。

企业级AI的特权地位？法院暗示不同条款的AI工具"可以被不同对待"，但没有明确裁定企业级AI一定受特权保护。这个问题可能需要另一个案件来回答。

AI幻觉（hallucination）的法律后果？如果当事人将AI生成的不准确信息提交给法院，可能面临Rule 11制裁。这一风险在没有律师审查的情况下尤其严重。

九结语：AI改变了一切，除了法律的底层逻辑

Heppner案最深刻的启示不是"不要用AI"——AI是不可阻挡的效率工具。它的启示是：法律的底层逻辑——保密性、信义义务、专业独立性——不会因为技术的变化而改变。

当你在AI的输入框里按下回车键的那一刻，你做出的不仅是一个技术选择，更是一个法律决定。这个决定的后果，可能在几个月后的法庭上才会显现。

"AI是你最聪明的助手，也可能是你最危险的证人。区别仅在于——你选择了什么工具，以及谁指导你使用它。"

English Version
⚖

Core Holding: Using Public AI Is Like Broadcasting Your Secrets

Judge Rakoff's logic is straightforward: anything you input into a public AI platform may, per the platform's privacy policy, be used for model training and disclosed to third parties — even government authorities. When the foundation of confidentiality is destroyed, attorney-client privilege and work product protection collapse with it.

IBackground: A CEO, a Fraud Scheme, and 31 AI Documents

Bradley Heppner was Chairman of GWG Holdings, a publicly traded New York company. In November 2025, he was indicted on multiple charges including securities fraud, wire fraud, conspiracy, lying to auditors, and record falsification. Prosecutors alleged he funneled money out of GWG Holdings through a shell company he controlled, Highland Consolidated Limited Partnership.

The case was assigned to Judge Jed S. Rakoff — one of the most senior and intellectually fearless judges on the Southern District bench.

The critical twist came when federal agents searched Heppner's devices and discovered 31 documents generated using Anthropic's Claude AI tool. The content was striking: after receiving a grand jury subpoena and retaining legal counsel, Heppner had independently used Claude to analyze his case, develop defense strategies, and evaluate legal arguments.

Heppner's defense team at Quinn Emanuel immediately asserted privilege and work product protection over all 31 documents, demanding their exclusion from evidence. The prosecution objected. Thus began the most significant legal privilege battle of the AI era.

IIIJudge Rakoff's Triple Rejection: Why Every AI Document Was Exposed

The defense advanced two protection theories: attorney-client privilege and work product doctrine. Judge Rakoff subjected each to rigorous legal testing — and struck down both.

🔒

Test 1: Attorney-Client Privilege — Confidentiality Requirement

The classic three-part test for attorney-client privilege: communications must be (1) among privileged parties only, (2) for the purpose of obtaining legal advice, and (3) kept confidential.

Judge Rakoff's fatal blow: by inputting information into Claude, Heppner "disclosed it to a third party." Anthropic's privacy policy explicitly states that inputs may be used for model training and may be disclosed to third parties and government regulatory authorities.

The judge's analogy was blunt: this is like discussing your case with friends at a bar — you cannot later claim those conversations are privileged.

❌ Confidentiality destroyed → Privilege fails
👥

Test 2: Attorney-Client Privilege — Communication Parties

Attorney-client privilege only protects communications between a client and their attorney. Judge Rakoff stated plainly: "Claude is not an attorney" — it lacks the fiduciary duties and professional discipline required for privilege. No "trusting human relationship" exists between users and AI platforms.

Critically, Claude itself disclaims: "I'm not a lawyer and can't provide formal legal advice." Even the AI doesn't consider itself a lawyer.

The defense argued these documents were created to "organize thoughts for communication with counsel," but the judge invoked the established principle: sending non-privileged documents to an attorney does not make them privileged. You cannot retroactively gild non-privileged materials by CC'ing your lawyer.

❌ Communication party disqualified → Privilege fails
📝

Test 3: Work Product Doctrine — Counsel Direction Requirement

Work product protection requires materials to be "prepared by or at the behest of counsel" in anticipation of litigation.

Heppner's fatal flaw: all 31 documents were generated entirely on his own initiative using Claude, with no evidence that his attorneys directed him to do so. He was simply a client who independently used an AI tool.

The court ruled: client-created documents, even if litigation-related, do not qualify as work product unless prepared under counsel's direction. An AI tool cannot substitute for the "direction" of an attorney.

⚠ No counsel direction → Work product protection inapplicable

"Inputting confidential information into a public AI platform is like displaying your defense strategy on a Times Square billboard — you can't later claim nobody saw it."

VConsumer AI vs. Enterprise AI: Same Technology, Worlds Apart

The Heppner ruling depends heavily on Anthropic's consumer-grade privacy policy. Judge Rakoff acknowledged that different AI tools with different terms "could be treated differently." This means the type of tool you use may directly determine whether privilege lives or dies.

🔴

Consumer AI (High Risk)

❌ Inputs may be used for model training
❌ Data may be shared with third parties
❌ Privacy policy permits government disclosure
❌ No contractual confidentiality obligations
❌ Heppner explicitly ruled unprotected
🟢

Enterprise AI (Safer)

✅ Contractual commitment: no training on inputs
✅ Data isolation; no third-party sharing
✅ Enterprise-grade encryption and access controls
✅ Can execute NDAs/DPAs
⚠ No case law confirming protection yet (but stronger position)

There's also a third option: locally deployed/edge-computing AI models (e.g., running open-source models on your own servers). Since data never leaves the internal network, there's no "third-party disclosure" issue — making it the strongest candidate for maintaining privilege.

VIAction Checklist for Lawyers, Clients, and Organizations

🚨

Stop entering confidential case information into public AI platforms immediately

This is the most direct lesson of Heppner. Whether you're a lawyer or client, entering any litigation- or investigation-related confidential information into ChatGPT, Claude public, Gemini, or similar consumer AI tools will almost certainly destroy privilege.

📜

Audit the privacy policy of every AI tool you use

Focus on three questions: Are inputs used for training? Could data be shared with third parties? Could data be disclosed to government agencies? If any answer is "yes," don't input confidential information.

🏢

Law firms and legal departments: procure enterprise AI tools

Enterprise APIs (Anthropic Enterprise, OpenAI Enterprise, Azure OpenAI) provide contractual confidentiality commitments. While no case law yet confirms enterprise AI privilege, the legal argument is far stronger than for consumer products.

📝

Create documented "AI usage directives" from counsel

If an attorney directs a client to use AI tools for material preparation, this must be documented in writing (email, memo). This is critical for work product protection — in Heppner, it was precisely the absence of evidence of counsel direction that defeated the claim.

📖

Update privilege logs to address AI tool usage

If you use AI tools in litigation, your privilege log should explicitly document: which AI tool was used, the tool's confidentiality commitments, whether use was at counsel's direction, and whether inputs contained attorney-client communications.

⚠

Warn clients: never input attorney advice into public AI

An often-overlooked risk: if a client "relays" their attorney's legal advice to a public AI for analysis, the originally privileged attorney-client communication may lose protection through "voluntary disclosure to a third party." This doesn't just expose the AI documents — it could retroactively destroy the privilege over the original attorney communications.

🔎

💻

Enterprise legal AI tools (Harvey, CoCounsel) gain a massive competitive advantage — because they're "confidential."

👥

🎒

AI privilege issues must become core content in legal ethics curricula. Lawyers need to understand technology to protect clients.

VIIIUnresolved Questions: The Gray Zones

While Heppner is a landmark ruling, it leaves several important questions unanswered:

Does the Kovel exception apply? Under established law, if an attorney directs a client to communicate with a third-party expert (such as an accountant or translator) to facilitate attorney-client communication, those third-party communications are also privileged (the Kovel exception). If counsel explicitly directs a client to use an AI tool to translate documents or analyze financial data, might Kovel protection apply? The court left this open.

Enterprise AI's privilege status? The court suggested that different AI tools with different terms "could be treated differently," but did not affirmatively rule that enterprise AI is privileged. This question likely awaits another case.

Legal consequences of AI hallucination? If a party submits AI-generated inaccurate information to a court, they may face Rule 11 sanctions. This risk is especially acute without attorney review.

IXConclusion: AI Changes Everything — Except the Law's Foundation

The deepest lesson of Heppner isn't "don't use AI" — AI is an unstoppable efficiency tool. The lesson is: the foundational principles of law — confidentiality, fiduciary duty, professional independence — do not bend to technological change.

The moment you press Enter in an AI input box, you're making not just a technology choice, but a legal decision. The consequences of that decision may only become visible months later in a courtroom.

"AI is your smartest assistant — and potentially your most dangerous witness. The difference lies entirely in what tool you chose, and who directed you to use it."
