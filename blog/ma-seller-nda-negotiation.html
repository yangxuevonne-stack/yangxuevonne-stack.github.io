卖方视角：如何在并购交易中谈判NDA保密协议

The Seller's NDA Playbook: Negotiating Confidentiality in M&A Transactions

九大核心条款 · 按风险等级从高到低 · 含谈判策略与建议条款语言Nine Key Clauses · Ranked by Risk · With Negotiation Strategies & Model Language
法律实务 · 约25分钟阅读Legal Practice · ~25 min readEvonne XuApril 2026
核心摘要Summary
并购交易中，买方通常提供NDA模板——这意味着卖方从一开始就处于被动地位。一份对卖方不利的NDA，可以在交易失败后将竞争对手变成知情者、将员工变成猎物、将卖方锁死在独家谈判中。本文从卖方律师视角，按风险等级从高到低，深度剖析九大核心NDA条款的买方陷阱、谈判策略与建议条款语言。 In M&A transactions, buyers typically supply the NDA template — putting sellers on the defensive from day one. A poorly negotiated NDA can turn competitors into insiders, employees into targets, and sellers into hostages locked in exclusive negotiations. This guide analyzes nine critical NDA clauses from the seller's perspective, ranked by risk level, with buyer traps, negotiation strategies, and model language for each.

☞ 卖方的特殊处境

签NDA，是把钥匙交给了可能的竞争对手

买方通常是战略竞争对手或有投资组合的PE——他们对你的商业模式、客户结构和员工团队充满兴趣，无论交易最终是否成交。NDA是你唯一的护城河，但许多卖方把它当成走流程的形式文件匆忙签署。

并购保密协议（NDA / Confidentiality Agreement）在交易生命周期中出现极早——通常在数据室开放、管理层会见、任何实质性披露之前。正因为此，大多数买方习惯提供一份"标准模板"，默认卖方会照单全收。

但对卖方而言，这份"标准模板"背后可能隐藏着四类核心风险：

🚫

商业秘密泄露

客户名单、定价策略、研发管线——一旦流入竞争对手，交易失败后你面对的是一个武装了你核心信息的对手。

🚫

关键员工被挖

买方在尽职调查中识别出你的核心团队，随后通过"合法渠道"发出offer——如果NDA没有禁止招揽条款或条款过弱，你的团队可能在交易失败后一个月内瓦解。

⚠

被锁死在单一谈判

停滞条款（Standstill）让卖方在独家谈判期内无法接触其他买家。一旦谈判破裂，卖方错过最佳窗口，估值预期大幅下滑。

⚠

违约风险倒置

救济条款（Remedies）通常只赋予买方单方面申请禁令救济的权利，卖方反而没有对等保护——这在双边NDA中极为不公平。

以下九大条款按风险等级从高到低排列，每个条款包含：风险评级、买方常见陷阱、卖方谈判策略，以及建议的条款语言。

条款风险等级核心问题
禁止招揽 / 竞业禁止极高交易失败后，管理团队被挖或创始人被迫退出行业
停滞条款极高独家期内无法接触其他买家，谈判筹码归零
机密信息的定义高定义过窄导致大量口头、视觉披露不受保护
救济措施高单边禁令权、无损害赔偿上限
允许的披露范围中Representative范围过宽，信息扩散无法控制
期限与存续期中统一短期限无法保护商业秘密
残留信息条款中允许买方"记住"并使用卖方核心信息
信息的归还与销毁较低无书面认证、允许保留电子备份
⚠ 高风险
条款一 · 机密信息的定义

Definition of Confidential Information

Confidential Information Definition

机密信息的定义是整个NDA的基石。定义越宽，卖方披露的信息获得的保护越全面；定义越窄，买方获得的"免费信息"越多。

买方——尤其是战略买方（Strategic Buyer）——在谈判这个条款时有明确的利益驱动：他们希望将定义限制在"书面标注为机密的文件"，从而让以下几类披露落入法外之地：

  • 管理层演示（Management Presentation）中的口头陈述
  • 工厂参观、技术展示中透露的运营信息
  • 初步接触阶段的电话和会议记录
  • 未经书面确认的客户名单、供应商信息
⚠ 典型买方陷阱

陷阱一："仅限书面标注为'机密'的文件"——口头披露、演示、参观全部游离在保护之外。

陷阱二：独立开发例外过宽——"买方独立开发的信息不受约束"，但如何证明"独立"？买方自己说了算。

陷阱三：公知例外用语模糊——"通过合法渠道可获得的信息"，买方可以主张任何已有文件记录的信息属于公知范畴。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ "仅指以书面形式提供且明确标注为CONFIDENTIAL的信息"
❌ 口头披露需在5个工作日内书面确认，否则不受保护
❌ 例外条款包含"买方可通过独立努力获得的信息"
❌ 未明确列举受保护信息类别（财务、客户、员工等）

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 定义涵盖"所有非公开信息，无论形式、标注与否"
✅ 对口头披露，确认期延长至10个工作日，且默认受保护
✅ 例外条款仅限于"已在公开记录中出现的信息"，且举证责任在买方
✅ 明确列举受保护类别：财务数据、客户名单、供应商关系、员工信息、技术规格、商业计划

建议条款语言

📝 Model Language — Confidential Information Definition
"Confidential Information" means all non-public information, data, analyses, compilations, studies, reports, or other materials relating to the Disclosing Party or its business, whether disclosed orally, in writing, electronically, visually (including during facility tours or management presentations), or in any other form, regardless of whether such information is labeled or identified as "confidential." For any oral or visual disclosures, the Disclosing Party may designate such information as Confidential Information by providing written confirmation within ten (10) Business Days of disclosure; provided, however, that failure to provide such written confirmation shall not automatically exclude such information from the protections of this Agreement if the context reasonably indicated its confidential nature. Confidential Information includes, without limitation: financial statements and projections, customer and supplier lists, pricing and cost data, employee information, technical specifications, intellectual property, business strategies and plans, and any information derived therefrom. Confidential Information does not include information that: (i) is or becomes generally publicly available through no fault of the Receiving Party; (ii) was rightfully known to the Receiving Party prior to disclosure, as evidenced by written records predating such disclosure; or (iii) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Confidential Information, as evidenced by contemporaneous written records. The burden of establishing any such exception shall rest with the Receiving Party.
🚫 极高风险
条款二 · 禁止招揽与竞业禁止

Non-Solicitation / Non-Compete

Non-Solicitation and Non-Compete Clauses

这是并购NDA中风险最高、卖方最常忽视的条款。许多卖方律师将注意力集中在保密义务本身，却对这两个条款掉以轻心——而这恰恰是买方最希望的。

⚠ 为什么战略买方格外在意这个条款

战略买方（尤其是直接竞争对手）在尽职调查阶段会系统性地识别卖方的关键员工。即便最终未成交，他们已经掌握了你的核心人才信息。禁止招揽条款是唯一的防火墙——但这把防火墙往往只对买方单向有利，卖方却浑然不觉。

禁止招揽（Non-Solicitation）vs. 竞业禁止（Non-Compete）

这两个条款经常被混为一谈，但法律效果和谈判策略截然不同：

⚠ 禁止招揽（较常见）

• 内容：禁止主动联系、招揽对方员工或客户
❌ 陷阱：措辞不当会变成"禁止任何雇佣"（含对方员工主动求职）
❌ 买方版本通常只约束卖方，对买方无对等限制
❌ 期限2-3年，远超行业惯例（12个月）

⚠ 竞业禁止（极其危险）

• 内容：禁止卖方创始人/管理层在特定期限、地域内从事同类业务
❌ 最大陷阱：在NDA阶段就签署竞业禁止——此时无任何对价，交易未必成交
❌ 地域"全球"或"任何竞争性业务所在地"
❌ "竞争性业务"定义模糊，可扩展解释
⚠ 卖方核心谈判原则

原则一：对等（Reciprocity）。如果买方要求卖方承担禁止招揽义务，则买方同样不得招揽卖方员工。

原则二：触发条件。禁止招揽义务应仅在交易最终交割后生效，而非在签署NDA时立即生效。

原则三：期限。禁止招揽不超过12个月；竞业禁止应在NDA阶段完全拒绝，留待签署正式协议（SPA）时单独谈判，并获得对价。

建议条款语言

📝 Model Language — Non-Solicitation (Reciprocal)
During the period commencing on the date hereof and ending twelve (12) months after the termination of discussions between the parties regarding the Transaction (the "Restricted Period"), neither party shall, directly or indirectly, solicit for employment or hire any employee of the other party who was introduced to or became known to such party in connection with the evaluation of the Transaction; provided, however, that the foregoing shall not prohibit either party from (i) general solicitations of employment not specifically targeted at such employees (including through search firms or job postings), (ii) hiring any person who approaches such party on an unsolicited basis, or (iii) hiring any person who has ceased to be employed by the other party for at least six (6) months prior to the date of hire. For the avoidance of doubt, this Section does not constitute a non-compete restriction and shall not be construed to prohibit either party from engaging in any business activities competitive with the other party.
🚫 极高风险
条款三 · 停滞条款

Standstill Provisions

Standstill Provisions

停滞条款（Standstill）是并购NDA中最容易被卖方忽视、但一旦激活就后果极为严重的条款。它的核心逻辑是：买方花费时间和资源进行尽调，要求在特定期间内卖方不得与其他买家接触，以保护买方的投入。

表面上看合情合理，但从卖方角度，这实际上是将最重要的谈判筹码——"我还有其他选择"——在NDA阶段就自愿交出去了。

⚠ 停滞条款的三大陷阱

陷阱一：出现在NDA而非LOI。停滞条款应出现在意向书（LOI）阶段，因为LOI明确了交易条款框架；若出现在NDA阶段，卖方在任何条款敲定之前就已经被锁定。

陷阱二：期限过长、无退出机制。许多买方要求6-12个月的独家期，且无明确的卖方单方终止权——即使买方多次拖延、不积极推进，卖方也没有出路。

陷阱三：没有"受托义务例外"（Fiduciary-Out）。对于上市公司或有受托义务的卖方主体，没有fiduciary-out意味着即使董事会收到更优厚的报价，也无法回应。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ 在NDA中即约定90天独家期
❌ 期间卖方不得接触、回应或鼓励任何第三方收购兴趣
❌ 无买方积极推进义务，无视买方拖延的后果
❌ 缺乏卖方终止权或fiduciary-out条款

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 首选：将停滞条款从NDA中完全删除，留待LOI单独谈判
✅ 如必须接受：独家期不超过30-45天，且须有明确的里程碑要求
✅ 加入买方积极推进义务：若买方未在X天内提交LOI/推进尽调，独家期自动终止
✅ 明确列出卖方可单方终止的触发条件（买方违约、主动降价、超时未推进）
✅ 对上市公司卖方，必须保留fiduciary-out条款
"停滞条款就像在谈判还没开始前先把自己的底牌翻开——买方知道你无处可去，压价便是水到渠成。" "A standstill at the NDA stage is handing your leverage to the buyer before negotiations even begin — they know you have nowhere else to go."
⚠ 高风险
条款四 · 救济措施

Remedies

Remedies for Breach

救济措施条款规定了NDA被违反时的法律后果。大多数NDA都会包含一段"承认货币损害难以量化，因此有权申请禁令救济"的语言——听上去无害，但对卖方而言可能是个陷阱。

🚫 救济条款的核心不对称

标准买方版本只赋予买方申请禁令救济的权利（因为买方是披露方）。但在双边NDA中（卖方也向买方披露信息），卖方理应享有对等的救济权。此外，无赔偿上限意味着一旦发生违约，卖方面对的是无限敞口——这在谈判层面对买方更有利，因为他们知道卖方在诉讼成本上的承受力更弱。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ 单边赋予买方（披露方）禁令救济权
❌ "无需提交担保即可申请禁令"（免去买方的诉讼成本风险）
❌ 无损害赔偿上限（Damages Cap）
❌ "不放弃追求任何其他救济"——让任何违约金条款形同虚设

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 要求禁令救济条款对双方对等适用
✅ 争取损害赔偿上限：建议为买方在交易中已支付费用的合理倍数，或约定固定金额
✅ 将禁令救济限定在"特定已识别机密信息被实际威胁披露"的情形，而非泛化到所有违约
✅ 如双边NDA，明确双方互享对等救济权
◈ 中等风险
条款五 · 允许的披露范围

Permitted Disclosures

Permitted Disclosures to Representatives

买方在收到卖方机密信息后，通常需要向内部团队和外部顾问共享，以开展尽职调查。允许的披露范围（Permitted Disclosures）条款界定了买方可以合法共享信息的人员边界。

买方的标准版本会将"代表方"（Representatives）定义得尽可能宽泛，理由是尽调需要——但这恰恰是信息扩散的入口。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ Representatives包括：关联公司（Affiliates）、潜在融资方、投资顾问、咨询公司
❌ "Affiliates"定义过宽，包含竞争对手的关联实体
❌ 允许向联合收购方（Consortium Members）共享，且无需卖方同意
❌ Representatives无需签署独立保密协议，买方仅承担"合理努力"的监督义务

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 要求所有接触机密信息的Representatives签署书面保密承诺，或受买方NDA义务的明确约束
✅ 明确排除：竞争对手及其关联公司（包括PE投资组合公司中的竞争对手）
✅ 向Consortium Members的披露须事先获得卖方书面同意
✅ 买方对其所有Representatives的违约行为承担连带责任
💡 实践提示

对于PE买方，要特别注意其投资组合（Portfolio Companies）中是否有你的直接竞争对手。PE基金的"Affiliates"通常包含数十家被投公司——如果NDA允许向"Affiliates"无限制披露，相当于向整个PE系的所有公司开放了你的商业秘密。

◈ 中等风险
条款六 · 期限与存续期

Term and Survival

Duration of Confidentiality Obligations

期限条款规定了保密义务持续多久。从卖方角度，核心问题是：买方获取的信息（尤其是商业秘密）价值往往超过2-3年，但买方通常坚持保密义务设有明确届满日。

⚠ 统一期限的危险

买方常提出"所有保密义务均在协议终止后2年届满"。但客户名单可能5年后仍然有效；研发管线可能是10年的战略资产；创始人的商业判断经验可能是卖方最有价值的无形资产。一刀切的短期限，等于在2年后向买方开放你的全部商业秘密。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ 统一2年保密期，覆盖所有类型信息
❌ 协议终止后买方所有义务随即消灭
❌ 未区分"一般机密信息"与"商业秘密"的保护期限

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 分层设计：一般机密信息3-5年；商业秘密无限期（或与法定保护期挂钩）
✅ 协议期满后归还/销毁义务仍然存续
✅ 明确定义何为"商业秘密"（符合DTSA定义），避免日后争议
◈ 中等风险
条款七 · 残留信息条款

Residuals Clause

Residuals / Unaided Memory Exception

残留信息条款（Residuals Clause）是并购NDA中最具争议、也最难谈判的条款之一。它起源于科技行业的知识产权协议，近年来被越来越多的买方（尤其是科技公司和大型PE）引入并购NDA。

条款的核心逻辑是：买方员工在接触卖方机密信息后，可能"无意识地"将这些信息留存于记忆中。Residuals条款主张，这类"无意识记忆"中的信息不受NDA约束，可以在未来的工作中自由使用。

🚫 实际执行中的巨大漏洞

"无意识"与"有意"之间的边界极难举证。一个买方员工在看完你的定价策略后，在脑中"记住"了关键数字，随后在竞争性产品定价中"恰好"采用了类似策略——你如何证明这不是"无意识残留"？在实践中，Residuals条款基本上是授予买方员工一张使用卖方核心信息的隐形许可证。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ "接触机密信息的人员以记忆形式保留的信息，不受本协议约束"
❌ 无需证明记忆是"无意识的"
❌ 无使用目的限制（可用于任何商业目的）
❌ 适用于所有信息类型，包括商业秘密和客户名单

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 首选：完全拒绝Residuals条款——这并非行业惯例，完全可以拒绝
✅ 如买方坚持，要求：仅限"真正无意识且未刻意记忆"的信息
✅ 明确排除：客户名单、定价数据、研发细节等已识别的核心商业秘密
✅ 限制使用目的：不得用于开发与卖方竞争的产品或服务
○ 较低风险
条款八 · 信息的归还与销毁

Return and Destruction of Information

Return or Destruction of Confidential Information

当交易谈判终止时，卖方有权要求买方归还或销毁所有机密信息。这个条款看似简单，但在执行层面存在若干值得关注的陷阱。

❌ 常见买方版本（陷阱）

❌ 允许保留"因技术原因无法删除的电子备份"（如自动存档系统）
❌ 仅要求"合理努力"销毁，无需完全彻底
❌ 允许代表方保留副本（Representatives通常不受卖方直接约束）
❌ 无书面认证要求，卖方无法核实是否真正完成销毁

✅ 卖方谈判策略

✅ 要求在交易终止后30个工作日内完成归还或销毁
✅ 销毁范围明确：包括所有电子文件、衍生材料、分析报告、笔记
✅ 要求买方授权高管提供书面销毁证明
✅ 技术备份例外需明确限制：不得被人工访问或用于商业目的

建议条款语言

📝 Model Language — Return and Destruction
Promptly following a written request by the Disclosing Party, or upon termination of discussions between the parties, the Receiving Party shall, within thirty (30) Business Days, at the Disclosing Party's election, either (i) return to the Disclosing Party all Confidential Information (including all copies, extracts, summaries, analyses, notes, and derivative materials thereof, in whatever form or medium) or (ii) destroy all such Confidential Information and certify such destruction in writing signed by an authorized officer of the Receiving Party. Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Receiving Party may retain copies of Confidential Information that are stored in automated backup or archiving systems in the ordinary course of business, provided that (x) such retained copies shall remain subject to all confidentiality obligations hereunder, (y) such retained copies shall not be accessed or used for any purpose, and (z) such retained copies shall be permanently deleted in accordance with the Receiving Party's standard data retention schedule. The obligations of the Receiving Party under this Agreement shall survive the return or destruction of Confidential Information.
综合策略 · 谈判全局观

卖方的NDA谈判总策略

The Seller's Overall NDA Negotiation Strategy

单个条款的谈判固然重要，但更关键的是卖方在整个NDA谈判过程中保持正确的战略姿态。

一、提供卖方版本，主动掌握主动权

谁提供模板，谁就在定义起点。如果等买方发来他们的"标准版本"再逐条反驳，卖方已经处于守势。准备一套卖方友好的NDA模板，在首轮接触时主动提供，是最有效的谈判策略之一。

二、在数据室开放前完成谈判

NDA在第一次实质性披露前必须完成谈判并签署。数据室一旦开放，卖方失去了最大的谈判筹码——因为买方知道你已经开始"卖"了。

💡 实践洞察

很多卖方急于推进交易，在NDA谈判上耗费的时间远少于后续条款谈判。但实际上，NDA阶段的让步往往奠定了整个交易谈判的基调——如果卖方在NDA上表现出"好说话"，买方会预期在LOI和SPA阶段同样可以步步紧逼。

三、区分红线与可接受条款

不是每个条款都值得死磕——但有些条款绝对不能妥协：

🚫

绝对红线

竞业禁止不得出现在NDA阶段；停滞条款须从NDA中删除；禁止招揽须对等。

⚠

重要谈判点

机密信息定义须宽泛；Representatives须签署保密承诺；商业秘密保护期须无限期。

✓

可以让步的条款

归还/销毁的具体期限（可以从30天谈到45天）；代表方的书面承诺形式（可以用买方对Representatives的连带责任代替）。

四、独立法律顾问不可或缺

即便是经验丰富的卖方管理层，也应当在签署NDA前委托独立法律顾问审阅。关键原因：

  • 律师能识别条款中的隐性陷阱（尤其是Residuals和Standstill）
  • 委托律师谈判可以将"强硬条款"与"个人关系"剥离——保留与买方的良好互动
  • 如日后发生争议，律师审阅记录（Legal Review）能证明卖方已尽职审查，有助于违约认定
💡 最终建议

NDA不是形式文件，是你在整个M&A过程中的第一道也是最重要的法律防线

当交易最终未能成交，NDA是唯一还站在你身边的合同。花时间谈好它，不只是为了保护这次交易——更是为了防止这次谈判本身成为你最大的竞争风险。

☞ Seller's Unique Position

Signing an NDA Means Handing Your Keys to a Potential Competitor

Buyers are often strategic competitors or PE firms with portfolio companies — deeply interested in your business model, customer relationships, and team, regardless of whether a deal closes. The NDA is your only moat, but most sellers treat it as procedural paperwork and sign without scrutiny.

M&A confidentiality agreements (NDAs / Confidentiality Agreements) appear early in the deal lifecycle — typically before the data room opens, before management meetings, and before any substantive disclosure. Because of this, buyers routinely offer their "standard form" expecting sellers to accept without negotiation.

But for sellers, four core risks lurk beneath that standard form:

🚫

Trade Secret Leakage

Customer lists, pricing strategies, R&D pipelines — once transferred to a competitor, deal failure leaves you facing an adversary armed with your core intelligence.

🚫

Key Employee Poaching

Buyers systematically identify top talent during due diligence. Without strong non-solicitation protections, your A-team may receive offers within weeks of deal failure.

⚠

Locked into a Single Negotiation

Standstill provisions prevent sellers from approaching other buyers during the exclusivity period. A broken deal means missed windows and compressed valuations.

⚠

Asymmetric Liability

Remedies clauses typically grant buyers unilateral injunctive relief rights — without equivalent protections for sellers in a mutual NDA framework.

The following nine clauses are analyzed in descending order of risk. Each includes a risk rating, common buyer traps, seller negotiation strategies, and where appropriate, model contract language.

Clause 1 · Confidential Information

Definition of Confidential Information

Breadth and Scope of the Definition

The definition of Confidential Information is the cornerstone of the entire NDA. A broad definition protects more of what sellers disclose; a narrow definition creates free zones buyers can exploit.

Strategic buyers have a clear incentive to narrow the definition to "documents marked 'CONFIDENTIAL' in writing," leaving the following unprotected:

  • Oral statements during management presentations
  • Operational information revealed during facility tours
  • Phone calls and meeting notes from early-stage discussions
  • Customer lists and supplier information shared without written confirmation
⚠ Classic Buyer Traps

Trap 1: "Only written materials explicitly marked CONFIDENTIAL" — oral disclosures, presentations, tours fall outside protection.

Trap 2: Overbroad independent development exception — "information independently developed by Receiving Party" is difficult to disprove.

Trap 3: Vague public domain exception — "information available through lawful means" lets buyers argue any documented information is public.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ "Only written materials explicitly labeled CONFIDENTIAL"
❌ Oral disclosures require written confirmation within 5 business days or lose protection
❌ Exceptions include "information obtainable through independent efforts"
❌ No enumeration of protected categories (financial, customer, employee data)

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Define as "all non-public information, regardless of form or labeling"
✅ Extend oral confirmation window to 10 business days, with default protection
✅ Limit exceptions to "publicly available in public records," burden on buyer to prove
✅ Enumerate protected categories: financial data, customer lists, supplier relationships, employee data, technical specs, business plans

Model Language

📝 Model Language — Confidential Information Definition
"Confidential Information" means all non-public information, data, analyses, compilations, studies, reports, or other materials relating to the Disclosing Party or its business, whether disclosed orally, in writing, electronically, visually (including during facility tours or management presentations), or in any other form, regardless of whether such information is labeled or identified as "confidential." For any oral or visual disclosures, the Disclosing Party may designate such information as Confidential Information by providing written confirmation within ten (10) Business Days; provided, however, that failure to provide such confirmation shall not automatically exclude information that was reasonably indicated to be confidential in context. Confidential Information includes, without limitation: financial statements and projections, customer and supplier lists, pricing and cost data, employee information, technical specifications, intellectual property, and business strategies. The burden of establishing any exception shall rest with the Receiving Party.
🚫 Critical Risk
Clause 2 · Non-Solicitation / Non-Compete

Non-Solicitation and Non-Compete Clauses

The Most Dangerous Clauses Sellers Overlook

These are the highest-risk clauses in M&A NDAs and the ones sellers most frequently overlook. While seller counsel focuses on confidentiality obligations, buyers quietly embed provisions that can cripple seller operations long after deal failure.

⚠ Why Strategic Buyers Care Most About These Clauses

Strategic buyers — especially direct competitors — systematically identify your key employees during due diligence. Even if the deal fails, they now know your A-team. Non-solicitation provisions are the only firewall — but they typically only protect the buyer, not the seller.

⚠ Non-Solicitation (Common)

❌ Trap: "No solicitation or hiring" — prohibits even responding to unsolicited applications
❌ Buyer version typically only restricts seller, with no reciprocal obligation on buyer
❌ Duration 2-3 years, far exceeding market standard (12 months)

⚠ Non-Compete (Extremely Dangerous)

❌ Biggest trap: signing non-compete at NDA stage — no consideration, no deal certainty
❌ Geographic scope "worldwide" or "any jurisdiction where competitive businesses operate"
❌ Vague definition of "competitive business" invites expansive interpretation
⚠ Core Seller Principles

Principle 1: Reciprocity. If buyer requires non-solicitation of seller's employees, buyer must be equally restricted from soliciting seller's employees.

Principle 2: Trigger conditions. Obligations should activate only upon deal closing, not upon NDA execution.

Principle 3: Timing. Non-solicitation: maximum 12 months. Non-compete: refuse entirely at NDA stage; negotiate separately in SPA with full consideration.

Model Language

📝 Model Language — Non-Solicitation (Reciprocal)
During the period commencing on the date hereof and ending twelve (12) months after the termination of discussions regarding the Transaction, neither party shall directly or indirectly solicit for employment or hire any employee of the other party who was introduced in connection with the Transaction evaluation; provided, however, that the foregoing shall not prohibit (i) general solicitations not specifically targeting such employees, (ii) hiring any person who approaches on an unsolicited basis, or (iii) hiring any person who has been separated from the other party for at least six (6) months. This Section does not constitute a non-compete restriction.
🚫 Critical Risk
Clause 3 · Standstill Provisions

Standstill Provisions

Exclusivity Restrictions That Lock Sellers In

Standstill provisions are the most underestimated clauses in M&A NDAs. Their surface logic is reasonable: buyers invest time and resources in due diligence and want assurance that sellers won't shop the deal simultaneously. But from the seller's perspective, accepting a standstill at the NDA stage means voluntarily surrendering the most important negotiating leverage — "I have other options."

⚠ Three Core Standstill Traps

Trap 1: Appearing in NDA instead of LOI. Standstill belongs in the Letter of Intent, where deal terms are established. In an NDA, sellers are locked in before any terms are agreed.

Trap 2: Long duration with no exit. Buyers request 6-12 months of exclusivity with no seller termination right — even if the buyer delays repeatedly and fails to progress.

Trap 3: No fiduciary-out. For publicly traded sellers or those with fiduciary duties, no fiduciary-out means the board cannot respond to superior proposals.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ 90-day exclusivity embedded in NDA
❌ Seller cannot contact, respond to, or encourage any third-party acquisition interest
❌ No buyer diligence milestones; buyer delays have no consequences
❌ No seller termination right or fiduciary-out

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Best outcome: Delete standstill entirely from NDA; defer to LOI stage
✅ If unavoidable: maximum 30-45 days with clear milestone requirements
✅ Add buyer diligence obligations: auto-terminates if buyer fails to submit LOI within X days
✅ List explicit seller termination triggers (buyer breach, price reduction, material delay)
✅ Mandatory fiduciary-out for public company sellers
"停滞条款就像在谈判还没开始前先把自己的底牌翻开——买方知道你无处可去，压价便是水到渠成。" "Agreeing to a standstill at the NDA stage is handing your leverage to the buyer before negotiations even begin — they know you have nowhere else to go."
⚠ High Risk
Clause 4 · Remedies

Remedies for Breach

Injunctive Relief and Damages Provisions

Remedies clauses specify the legal consequences of NDA breach. Most NDAs include language acknowledging that monetary damages are difficult to quantify, therefore entitling the aggrieved party to injunctive relief. Sounds neutral — but the asymmetry in how this is applied creates significant risk for sellers.

🚫 The Core Asymmetry

Standard buyer templates grant only the buyer unilateral injunctive relief rights (as the party "disclosing" information for due diligence purposes). But in a mutual NDA where sellers also disclose information, sellers deserve equivalent remedies. Furthermore, no damages cap creates unlimited liability exposure — buyers know sellers have less capacity to sustain litigation costs.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ Injunctive relief granted unilaterally to buyer only
❌ "Without requirement of posting bond or other security"
❌ No damages cap — unlimited liability for any breach
❌ "Without waiving any other available remedies" — renders liquidated damages clauses meaningless

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Require reciprocal injunctive relief rights for both parties
✅ Negotiate a damages cap: typically a fixed amount or multiple of fees paid
✅ Limit injunctive relief to specific, identified information under actual threat of disclosure
✅ In mutual NDAs, make explicit that both parties have symmetric remedy rights
◈ Medium Risk
Clause 5 · Permitted Disclosures

Permitted Disclosures to Representatives

Controlling Information Dissemination

After receiving seller's confidential information, buyers need to share it internally and with external advisors to conduct due diligence. The Permitted Disclosures clause defines the permissible scope of such sharing. Buyers draft this as broadly as possible — but breadth creates uncontrolled information spread.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ "Representatives" includes Affiliates, potential financing sources, investment advisors, consultants
❌ "Affiliates" is overbroad — includes competitor portfolio companies of PE buyers
❌ Disclosure to consortium members without seller consent
❌ Representatives need not sign separate NDAs; buyer only owes "reasonable efforts" to ensure compliance

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Require all Representatives accessing confidential information to execute written confidentiality commitments, or be expressly bound by the NDA terms
✅ Explicitly exclude competitors and their affiliates (including PE portfolio companies)
✅ Disclosure to consortium members requires prior written seller consent
✅ Buyer assumes joint liability for all Representative breaches
💡 Practical Note on PE Buyers

For PE buyers, scrutinize whether their portfolio companies include direct competitors. A PE fund's "Affiliates" may encompass dozens of portfolio companies — an unrestricted disclosure right to Affiliates effectively opens your trade secrets to the entire portfolio ecosystem.

◈ Medium Risk
Clause 6 · Term and Survival

Term and Survival of Obligations

Duration of Confidentiality Obligations

Term clauses define how long confidentiality obligations persist. The core seller concern: the value of disclosed information — especially trade secrets — often extends well beyond 2-3 years, but buyers routinely insist on a fixed expiry date.

⚠ The Danger of Uniform Duration

Buyers often propose "all confidentiality obligations expire 2 years after termination." But a customer list may remain valuable for 5 years; an R&D pipeline may be a 10-year strategic asset; a founder's market judgment may be the most valuable intangible. A blanket short duration effectively opens all your trade secrets to the buyer after 2 years.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ Uniform 2-year duration covers all information types
❌ All obligations extinguish upon agreement termination
❌ No distinction between general confidential information and trade secrets

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Tiered approach: general confidential information 3-5 years; trade secrets perpetual (or tied to statutory protection period)
✅ Return/destruction obligations survive agreement expiration
✅ Define "trade secrets" expressly (per DTSA standards) to prevent future disputes
◈ Medium Risk
Clause 7 · Residuals Clause

Residuals Clause

The "Unaided Memory" Exception

The Residuals Clause is one of the most contested provisions in M&A NDAs. Originating in tech industry IP agreements, it has migrated into acquisition NDAs — particularly those drafted by technology companies and large PE funds.

The core argument: after reviewing confidential information, buyer employees may "inadvertently" retain information in memory. The Residuals Clause asserts that information retained through unaided human memory is not subject to NDA restrictions.

🚫 The Proof Problem

The line between "inadvertent" and "deliberate" memory is virtually impossible to police. A buyer employee who reads your pricing strategy and "happens to remember" the key figures when pricing a competitive product — how do you prove that wasn't "unaided memory"? In practice, the Residuals Clause is essentially a hidden license to use seller's core information.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ "Information retained in the unaided memories of those who accessed Confidential Information shall not be subject to this Agreement"
❌ No requirement to prove memory was inadvertent
❌ No restriction on use purpose — may be used for any commercial activity
❌ Applies to all information types, including trade secrets and customer lists

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Best outcome: Reject Residuals entirely — this is not standard in all M&A contexts
✅ If buyer insists: limit to "genuinely inadvertent and not deliberately memorized" information
✅ Explicitly exclude: customer lists, pricing data, R&D details, and identified trade secrets
✅ Restrict use: may not be used to develop products or services competitive with seller
○ Lower Risk
Clause 8 · Return and Destruction

Return and Destruction of Information

Post-Termination Information Handling

When deal discussions terminate, sellers have the right to demand return or destruction of all confidential information. While seemingly straightforward, execution-level traps can undermine this protection.

❌ Typical Buyer Version (Traps)

❌ Permitted to retain electronic backups "not reasonably practicable to delete"
❌ "Reasonable efforts" to destroy — not complete destruction
❌ Representatives may retain their own copies outside seller's direct reach
❌ No written certification requirement — no verification of actual destruction

✅ Seller Negotiation Strategy

✅ Require completion within 30 business days of deal termination
✅ Scope includes all electronic files, derivative materials, analyses, and notes
✅ Require written destruction certificate signed by an authorized buyer officer
✅ Backup retention exception must expressly prohibit human access or commercial use

Model Language

📝 Model Language — Return and Destruction
Promptly following a written request by the Disclosing Party, or upon termination of discussions between the parties, the Receiving Party shall, within thirty (30) Business Days, at the Disclosing Party's election, either (i) return to the Disclosing Party all Confidential Information (including all copies, extracts, summaries, analyses, notes, and derivative materials thereof) or (ii) destroy all such Confidential Information and certify such destruction in writing signed by an authorized officer. Retained automated backup copies shall remain subject to all confidentiality obligations, shall not be accessed or used for any purpose, and shall be permanently deleted in accordance with the Receiving Party's standard data retention schedule. The obligations of the Receiving Party under this Agreement shall survive the return or destruction of Confidential Information.
Overall Strategy

The Seller's NDA Negotiation Playbook

Strategic Positioning Across the Full Negotiation

Individual clause negotiations matter — but the seller's strategic posture across the entire NDA process is equally critical.

I. Provide Your Own Template — Own the Starting Position

Whoever provides the template defines the starting point. Waiting for the buyer's "standard form" and then arguing line by line puts sellers on defense. Preparing a seller-friendly NDA template and offering it at first contact is one of the most effective opening moves available.

II. Complete Negotiations Before Opening the Data Room

The NDA must be fully negotiated and executed before any substantive disclosure. Once the data room opens, sellers lose their most important leverage — buyers know the seller has begun the process of selling.

💡 Practice Insight

Many sellers eager to advance a deal spend far less time on NDA negotiations than on subsequent term sheets and SPAs. But concessions at the NDA stage set the tone for the entire transaction — a seller who appears "easy" on the NDA signals to buyers that further pressure will be equally rewarded.

III. Know Your Red Lines and Acceptable Trades

🚫

Absolute Red Lines

Non-compete must not appear at NDA stage. Standstill must be removed from the NDA. Non-solicitation must be reciprocal.

⚠

Important Negotiating Points

Confidential Information definition must be broad. Representatives must execute written commitments. Trade secret protection must be perpetual.

✓

Acceptable Concessions

Specific return/destruction timelines (30 to 45 days). Form of Representative commitments (direct signatures vs. buyer joint liability). Minor carve-outs to definition for truly public information.

💡 Final Recommendation

The NDA Is Not a Formality — It Is Your First and Most Important Legal Defense in the Entire M&A Process

When a deal fails to close, the NDA is the only contract still standing between you and the information you shared. Invest the time to negotiate it properly — not just to protect this transaction, but to prevent the negotiation itself from becoming your greatest competitive liability.
